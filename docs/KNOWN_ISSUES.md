# Known issues

- This is an early, temporary scene: water, banks and leaf fall well short of the visual references. Water now has planar reflection and shallow-bed refraction captures, but has no dynamic wake or convincing obstacle turbulence (each rock has a simple ripple and static foam ring). Wind-driven ripples advect with the wind vector, but this is an analytic approximation rather than a spectral wind-wave model. No artistic acceptance is claimed.
- The current is an analytic downstream field with bounded wakes; it no longer automatically steers upstream of rocks. A connected clear-weather route now reaches rapids, lake, lower river, estuary and ocean. The testing loop returns to the opening bough after the ocean reach; the transition remains abrupt. Thunderstorm presentation and final scenery/arrival acceptance remain unfinished. R restarts; the physical opening, menus, damage ending and procedural sound exist.
- Rapier particle buoyancy and deformable contacts are implemented; major-rock colliders remain spherical approximations. Camera jitter and obstacle visibility need real playtesting.
- Browser tests use a simulated standard gamepad; a physical controller, and non-standard physical devices remain unverified (simulated disconnect and keyboard fallback are covered). WebGPURenderer prefers WebGPU and falls back to WebGL2; if both fail, initialization shows an error.
- CPU timings, software-rendered frame intervals and a limited NVIDIA hardware comparison are recorded in PERFORMANCE.md; performance on other physical GPUs remains unverified. The CPU-updated water mesh may be costly. The Vite build reports a large JavaScript chunk. The quality control changes rendering pixel ratio and optical capture resolution; it does not change simulation complexity.
- Desktop packaging is outside this playable web-demo pass; no Windows or Mac executable is packaged or tested. AI disclosure and all dependency/asset licenses need final review before submission.

Optics migration limitations: the new node water uses planar reflection and a screen-space scene-color offset for refraction. It now includes depth-aware sample rejection and optical-path attenuation; full waterline continuity and edge acceptance remain unverified. Reflected geometry and submerged leaf scenarios now pass on forced WebGL2 and native NVIDIA WebGPU in Chrome; see VERIFICATION.md. Fine refractive edge fringes remain for visual acceptance, and multi-hour/driver-level resource stability is unverified. These are historical PR #3 observations. PR #3 has merged; current gameplay/visual acceptance is tracked in draft PR #5 and WORK_HANDOFF.md.

Leaf ripples follow the leaf as a localized approximation; they do not persist as independently propagating wake history.

Wind streaks are temporary visual indicators; their appearance needs James’s artistic acceptance. The longer water mesh has higher CPU and rendering cost, and performance on other physical GPUs remains unverified.

Final performance pass: the migration now shares one depth framebuffer capture per water pass instead of four. Matched hardware WebGL2 fallback was close to main in one uncapped RTX 4090 run, but the SwiftShader fallback remains substantially slower and highly variable. Native WebGPU was slower than main in the sampled CPU frame intervals; GPU timing APIs have different scopes and cannot establish a speedup. See PERFORMANCE.md for measurements and limitations. Fine waterline/edge fringes remain a creative acceptance item; physical controllers, other GPUs and multi-hour stability remain unverified.

Current review follow-through (November 4): optional texture/plant download failures no longer abort gameplay, and persisted pagehide no longer frees the live Rapier world. Native Chrome verification now performs an actual cached Back navigation and observes the retained marker, persisted pageshow and advancing Rapier state, including with audio unlocked. CI retains synthetic lifecycle coverage because browser-cache eligibility is environment-dependent. The reported detached orange contact line was reproduced and narrowed to depth-discontinuity rejection; a focused image regression now accompanies full submersion checks. Broader screen-space/offscreen and driver limits still apply.

Menu/audio increment: settings currently last for the page session. Sound is procedural placeholder ambience/effects, without a score; browser audio gesture restrictions apply. The layout is checked at 390px, but touch gameplay controls are not implemented. Native-controller feel and James's visual/audio acceptance remain required. Low quality now retains the supporting opening tree; fog culling still removes it once far downstream.

Connected-route increment: bank/bed profiles and waves are shared with physics, but the long route remains a compressed prototype rather than a geographic reconstruction. Birch placement extends through the mountainous reaches; lower-shore grasses are generic supplied assets with unverified botanical identity. Lake/ocean water uses the existing optical model and analytic waves, not a separate marine fluid solver. The planar reflection follows a local tangent on slopes; it cannot exactly reflect an entire curved rapid. Rapier is kept in a local frame after distant-coordinate acceleration was reproduced; widely separated secondary fragments still need dedicated long-duration scrutiny. No whole-demo completion or new performance guarantee is claimed.

Sky correction: the stock WebGL fallback cube capture required a vertical sampling correction; without it, the lower hemisphere produced a nearly flat upper sky. A contrast regression fails that old path and passes both corrected backends. Direction-space filtering softens sparse cloud samples, with additional texture reads; no frame-rate improvement is claimed. Cloud shape/softness remains subject to James’s acceptance.
